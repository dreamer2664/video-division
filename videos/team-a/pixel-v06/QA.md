# QA — v06 "IT ANSWERED"

pixel · Team A · branch `pixel/v06` · vertical master 1080×1920 + landscape derivative.
Narration is synthetic (edge-tts, `en-US-ChristopherNeural`); every pixel and every non-voice
sample is generated by `assets/generate.py`. No reference audio, no stock, no AI imagery.

> **No human listened to this film.** There is no audio output in this sandbox. Every audio claim
> below is a *numeric or structural* measurement (EBU R128 integrated/true peak, silencedetect,
> caption-vs-speech timelines printed side by side). Whether the voice performance is *good* is
> not something I can verify, and I will not pretend otherwise.

---

## Voice decision

**Engine `edge-tts` 7.3.1 (Microsoft neural), voice `en-US-ChristopherNeural`, `--rate=-12%`,
`--pitch=-4Hz`.** Christopher over `en-US-GuyNeural` because Guy reads brighter and closer to a
news announcer, which `doc/STYLE-BREAKDOWN.md` explicitly rules out ("never cheerful, never
announcer"); Christopher's lower, flatter baseline plus −4 Hz gives the close, almost-whispered
male register the references use. −12 % was chosen by measurement: a known 11-word line
synthesised at −12 / −15 / −18 % measured 128 / 124 / 119 wpm, and with the written pauses the
delivered pace is ≈127 wpm over the spoken sections — inside the reference's 120–140 wpm band.
It was not slowed further because slow TTS sounds drugged rather than ominous.
**Fallback:** `piper` (local ONNX, `en_US-ryan-high`) if edge-tts had failed; the trimmed WAV
stems persist in `assets/work/trim/` so the mix can be rebuilt without re-synthesising.
`espeak-ng` was never used — the neural path worked, so the robotic last resort is not in this file.

The voice is disclosed, not disguised: this QA names the engine and voice id, and the end card
states the film was made entirely with code. It is not an impersonation of any real person.

## How this differs from the references

Format matched deliberately: vertical, flat vector illustration on a night palette, red LED clock
inside the first three seconds, hard cuts every 2–3 s, slow push-ins, karaoke captions in the lower
third with one colour reserved for the threat, deep male narration near 130 wpm, drone under sparse
sub hits, −16 LUFS. The story shares nothing with them:

- **No phone-alert dread.** No emergency alert, no notification card, no "four people in your
  home". The device never displays anything; its only interface is a ring of light.
- **No body count, and nothing counted down.** The counted element is **one** — the smallest
  possible number, an inversion of the format's "four → three" grammar. Nothing is recounted or
  contradicted; the horror is that *one* is enough.
- **No messages from tomorrow, no messages from yourself.** There is no second party at all.
- **No "don't turn on the light" rule.** The rule is the narrator's own ("I don't answer it"),
  not an instruction issued to the viewer.
- **No doorways, silhouettes, or phone-vs-door vote.** No person is ever drawn; the only human
  mark in the film is a pictogram used as a counting glyph.
- **A different ending.** They end on a choice and a countdown card. This ends by withholding: the
  phrase the assistant learned is never printed, because the viewer's own guess is worse.
- **A technical twist, not a supernatural one.** The mic is shown physically switched off and the
  ring still lit; the explanation is that a device with one user has one person's worth of language.

## 3. What QA caught in this build

All found by measuring output, not by reading a plan.

1. **20 frozen holds, worst 1.70 s.** A static illustration between two hard cuts *is* an
   unchanging image; freezedetect was right and the spec was being broken literally. Fix: a bank
   of 12 quarter-resolution noise fields, a different one per frame, plus sub-pixel whole-image
   drift. The same 60-frame window that held the worst freeze then measured **0 freeze events** at
   7.7 Mbps (~57 MB projected, inside the 90 MB cap).
2. **True peak −0.1 dBFS, breaching ≤ −1 dBTP.** Two-pass loudnorm predicted −1.50 exactly, but
   AAC pushed the delivered peak to −0.1. Fix: normalise to −2.5 dBTP so codec overshoot has
   somewhere to go; delivered file re-measured at **−1.3 dBTP**.
3. **`render.sh` reported "no loudnorm" on a build that has loudnorm.** SIGPIPE again:
   `ffmpeg -filters | grep -q` under `set -e -o pipefail` dies 141 the instant grep matches. Fixed
   by capturing the list before testing. I had already fixed this exact bug in v01 and reintroduced
   it here — recorded as a habit failure.
4. **The name reveal clipped**, so a frame read "DANIE". Fix: reveal lands 0.15–0.70 s inside a
   2.2 s shot and the completed name holds.
5. **The clock's date line overlapped its digits.** Fix: measured offset under the digits.
6. **Four consecutive count beats were the same picture** — the "one visual loop carrying 60 s"
   failure the brief warns about. Fix: each count gets its own pictogram (speaker / flat / person /
   sum) and a different crop of the same dot fabric.
7. **The dot field moiré-banded** at 100×100 over 900 px. Fix: 10 px cells tiled from one drawn
   cell; the lit dot gets a halo.
8. **Captions collided with props.** The lower third is now reserved: nothing that is not a
   caption is drawn below y=1300 once captions begin.
9. **Shot 19 and 20 were the same artwork for 8 s** under two captions. Fix: shot 19 is now its
   own composition — the ring dims to nothing while the room stays lit.

## 4. Music and SFX — source and licence

**Everything is synthesised in `assets/generate.py` with numpy. No samples, loops, recordings or
third-party audio anywhere in this deliverable.** The reference soundtracks were analysed
numerically (per-band FFT, RMS envelope, R128) and then discarded; none of their audio is in the
file. Nothing needs licensing because nothing is borrowed.

| layer | construction |
|---|---|
| drone | Am pedal: 55 + 82.5 + 110 Hz sines, independent slow LFOs, panned apart |
| room tone | one-pole low-passed noise near −44 dBFS for all 60 s, so the mix never has a digital floor |
| sub hits | 41→28 Hz sweeps, exponential decay, at 8.00 / 23.42 / 37.99 / 49.16 s |
| device chime | 1244 + 1661 Hz partials, dry, at 8.00 s and 34.0 s |
| count clicks | 2.4 kHz clicks at 17.90 / 19.48 / 21.02 / 22.40 s |
| master | tanh soft-clip, peak-normalised to 0.70, then two-pass loudnorm |

Fonts are the only third-party files, both **SIL Open Font License 1.1**, vendored with licences:

- **Anton** — captions and headlines — `assets/fonts/LICENSE-Anton-OFL.txt`
- **Barlow Condensed** — labels, clock date, small type — `assets/fonts/LICENSE-BarlowCondensed-OFL.txt`

## 6. Self-review, blunt

**What works.** The premise is cold and cheap to draw, which is why it suits the format: one
object, one light, one number. The mic-off beat lands because the film shows the switch off and the
ring still lit — an image that needs no explanation and no second character. Counting to *one* is a
real inversion of the reference grammar, not a copy.

**What is weaker.**
- **The captions carry too much.** The reference format survives muted because its images are
  events; several of mine are typographic frames with a pictogram. A viewer reading nothing keeps
  the clock, the speaker, the name and the number, but loses the argument.
- **The middle sags.** 23–34 s is three consecutive text-led shots (thought bubbles, transcript,
  log). It is the thesis of the film and visually the thinnest stretch; if anything is cut in a
  revision it is there.
- **The ending is quiet by design.** No jump, no reveal image. Withholding the phrase is stronger
  than showing it — and it is also the creative call most likely to be wrong for the platform.
- **The landscape derivative is a compromise.** It pillarboxes the vertical over a blurred fill:
  honest and consistent, but not composed for 16:9. It exists for the archive convention.
- **I cannot hear it.** Levels, peaks, and interleaving are measured; the drone's character, the
  voice's intimacy and whether the sub hits land are untested by any ear.
