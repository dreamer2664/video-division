## What changed from v06 — the critique, taken literally

Manager A's note: *"too many of your frames are an object on near-empty darkness — the reference
videos always give you a place... the best script of the three and the least world."* That is
correct, and it is a production failure, not a taste difference: I had been drawing **objects**
(speaker, switch, log) on black, and using type as a substitute for scenery.

**v07 is a room.** Six fixed views of the same flat, reused and re-lit as the night goes wrong:
the **window** (street, rain, a lit window opposite), the **door** (hallway light under it), the
**table** (speaker and lamp on it), the **wall** (the speaker's ring thrown across it as a stain of
light), the **sofa**, and the **kitchen counter**. Every shot has foreground, midground, background,
and its own light source. The speaker is never alone on a void again.

Second note: *"cutting is too even."* v07's shot list is deliberately uneven — **seven shots under
2 s** (0.6–1.5 s) and **one 6-second hold** immediately before the twist, where the camera does not
cut at all and only rain moves. The average is 2.6 s but no two neighbours share a length.

## The premise (night two)

Night one established the speaker said a name it was never told. Night two: the flat is now
**listening back**. Every surface has become a microphone — the window glass, the door, the table.
The counted element this time is the floorplan itself: **six rooms/surfaces, six microphones**,
and the count climbs to a seventh that is not on the plan.

The rule, stated in second person: *if the flat is quiet, it can hear the room; so keep the flat
loud.* The twist: the noise was never hiding me from it — **it was hiding me from the room**.

Hook in my own words (not the reference's "FOLLOW FOR NIGHT 2"): **"It heard the quiet. — TOMORROW,
THE QUIET ANSWERS."**

resolves. Music 8–12 dB under the voice. Synthesised entirely in numpy — no samples.

## Numbers, and why they are honest

- **Six rooms, six surfaces, six microphones** — drawn as exactly six marks on the floorplan, each
  at one of the six views the film actually shows. The count is verifiable against the shot list.
- **The seventh mark** appears at 27.3 s, in a room that is *not* one of the six; the floorplan
  drawing adds precisely one rectangle at that moment, and the caption says seven.
- **Night two** is the same clock as night one: 2:14. Not a countdown.
