# PERFECT TIME — reference and design notes (pre-picture)

## Provenance, not a repeated study
This design adapts the existing v07 frame/caption study, committed as `fd49ec6815da3f594d8d58f13a61ad3fa53f5719` and recorded in that branch's `references/NOTES-reel.md`; it was not repeated for v09. In that study 5,714 archived frames were decoded for machine analysis, while selected original frames and contact sheets were inspected. Reference 01/02/03 contained 2,505/1,800/1,409 frames. No human listened to the reference soundtracks, and no reference image, audio, plot or dialogue will be used in this production.

The earlier study found dark edges, a large central visual idea, lower-centre outlined captions, and short rhythmic beats, often 2–4 words held roughly 0.6–1.8 s. In its measured examples, progressively emphasized words remained legible against a dark field. A one-point hallway established real depth while keeping the frame visually simple. v07's designed exit log and v08's disagreeing hospital screens demonstrate how consistent, believable UI furniture can carry a story. Both are engineering/format references, not reusable story or audio.

## Original v09 application
A fictional after-hours call centre reports `WAITING 01`, a perfect **00:40 average handling time**, and resolved callers whose calls remain **LIVE**. The queue replaces each finished caller and therefore never reaches zero. The fiction reveals that the system measures the caller's disappearance, not a disconnected call. The new final hook is a warning to the next voice, in original words. Distinct screens include a queue board, AHT metric, line-status grid, caller record, signal/waveform, headset timer, agent handoff and name prompt. Adjacent shots should change meaningful screen content or framing, not merely reset a zoom.

Create an original deep-blue/indigo night palette with cyan, warm amber and a controlled warning red; use compact screen chrome, large readable values and little decorative copy. One original scene must use a true one-point-perspective view down rows of call-centre desks/headsets, rather than a flat interface card. The landscape reframe must retain important UI without shrinking it to unreadable size, and reburn the same captions.

## Cut and caption beat plan
Thirty shots repeat **54 / 60 / 66 frames** ten times at 30 fps: **1.8 / 2.0 / 2.2 s**, exactly 60 s and a **2.0 s** mean. Caption groups target **2–5 words** with a full, deliberate pre-reveal pause; a separate SRT covers muted viewing. Per-word karaoke must be based on the narration's measured Edge word-boundary telemetry, not character-count or proportional estimates. ASS centiseconds and 30 fps introduce quantization, so audit both logical starts and the first eligible frame rather than claiming perfect phonetic alignment. Captions are centred with visible margins and a dark outline.

The original screenplay and this study are committed before any candidate v09 production picture is generated. The 60-second export will include an original newly synthesized voice stem, drone and call-centre Foley; exact production and perceptual limits will be reported from measured results, not assumed.
