## 3. What QA actually caught (and what I did about it)

This section exists because the first render looked fine and was not.

### 3.1 Black frames at 0.0–0.53 s and 50.4–50.93 s

`blackdetect` classifies a frame as black when ≥ 98 % of pixels fall under `pix_th` (default 0.10,
i.e. luma ≈ 25.5). My palette used a background of `#10151D` — luma ≈ 20.3, vignetted to ≈ 16 in
the corners. It *looked* like a deep charcoal, and to the detector it was black. The opening hold
and a quiet beat in S7 were therefore 0.53 s "black frames" by the spec's own measurement.

**Fix:** lifted the base to `#1A2433` (graphite navy, luma ≈ 28.7) and flattened the vignette from
0.35 to 0.16 so no corner dips under the threshold. Measured after: **0.00 %** of pixels below 25.5
on every probe frame, and `blackdetect` reports nothing.

### 3.2 Frozen frames — nine holds of 0.6–1.7 s

Typographic films hold type. `freezedetect` measures *no change at all*, so a static end card or a
held list is formally a frozen frame even though every pixel is deliberate. The first render had
holds at 2.8–3.5, 4.87–6.03, 6.6–7.23, 19.37–20.07, 20.77–22.03, 24.23–25.0, 25.5–26.33,
26.83–28.03, 39.0–40.03, 53.83–54.57 and **55.3–57.03 s** (the end of S7) — up to 1.73 s of
literally identical frames. Meeting the spec's letter *and* its intent meant the picture had to
keep breathing during holds.

**Fix, in two parts:**
1. **Living texture.** Every frame now receives a drifting light field (two seeded Gaussian glows
   on a slow Lissajous path, plus a faint radial phase drift) and film grain. Grain is generated at
   quarter resolution and upscaled so its structure sits at 2–4 px — x264 preserves that cheaply,
   where per-pixel noise cost 11 Mbps of bitrate fighting the encoder.
2. **A second-level motion.** A whole-film seconds bar was added at the top of frame: elapsed in
   amber, remaining in graphite, with a travelling spark. It is always moving, so no hold is dead,
   and it restates the film's subject in every frame — 60 s of seconds, counted.

**Calibration, not guesswork.** I rendered the four worst hold windows at several grain strengths
and measured freeze events and bitrate at CRF 18:

| σ | worst-window bitrate | freeze events | projected file |
|---|---|---|---|
| per-pixel noise, σ 2.3 | 11.4 Mbps | 0 | ~85 MB — too close to the 90 MB ceiling |
| quarter-res, σ 1.1 | 0.9 Mbps | **2** | grain quantised away, holds froze again |
| quarter-res, σ 2.6 | 8.5–9.4 Mbps | 0 | ~64 MB |
| **quarter-res, σ 2.0 (shipped)** | **7.2–8.1 Mbps** | **0** | **~55 MB** |

Final film: no `freeze_start` anywhere, 55.3 MiB.

### 3.3 Headline collisions at five shot hand-offs

The contact sheet (one frame every 2 s, §5) caught a frame at ~8.1 s where two different sentences
were superimposed at the same baseline, mid-crossfade, producing unreadable type. The cause was a
pattern, not a one-off: outgoing and incoming headings faded through each other *at the same y* in
five places (S2 2.6–3.2, S3 6.0–6.9 and 8.0–8.8, S5 9.3–10.2, S6 0.9–1.5).

**Fix:** rewrote all five as sequential hand-offs — the outgoing line finishes before the incoming
one starts. Verified frame by frame at 8.1 / 8.3 / 8.5 s and in the S5/S6 equivalents.

### 3.4 A false negative that would have blocked the run

`render.sh`'s encoder check was `ffmpeg -hide_banner -encoders | grep -q libx264`. With `pipefail`
set, `grep -q` exiting on its first match closes the pipe, FFmpeg dies of SIGPIPE, the pipeline
returns 141, and the script reported "ffmpeg lacks libx264" on a build that has it. The check was
fixed to capture the encoder list first. Worth recording: the tool was fine; the test was lying.

### 3.5 A self-caught fact error in the script

The arithmetic in the S5 landing was right (2026-10-31 minus 10⁹ s) but I had written "March 1995"
in `script.md` while the code computed 21 February 1995. On-screen copy only ever claimed "1995".
The script now says **21 February 1995** with a note about the slip, and `generate.py` asserts the
date at startup on every run, so the error cannot return silently.

## 4. Watched start to finish

I cannot play the file with sound in this sandbox, so "watch" means: every frame of `video.mp4`
decoded, sampled into two 15-tile contact sheets at 2 s intervals (0–28 s, 30–58 s), plus
full-resolution inspection of the first frame, the last frame, all eight shot transitions, and each
captioned beat. Findings are §3.3 (fixed) and the notes below.

- **First second (0.0–1.0 s):** background and the seconds bar at zero with its spark; two counters
  starting from 0 at 11 px/s of type — the film opens on counting, not on a title.
- **Last second (59.0–60.0 s):** end card holds; the seconds bar reaches its right terminus exactly
  at 60.0 s, which is the closing argument in one pixel row.
- **Transition discipline:** 0.4 s dissolves between shots, except the S4 legend flip, which is a
  hard cut on purpose — the flip is the turn of the film.
- **The turn (S4, 22–28 s):** "Now keep the same picture." ×1,000 slam; the legend snaps from
  100 to 100,000 seconds while the dot count does not change.

Bluntly: **I have not verified the audio by ear.** Levels, peaks, LUFS, LRA, and the absence of
silence holes are measured (§2), and the score is deterministic numpy synthesis with no samples to
be mis-timed, but whether the mix *feels* right is a judgement I cannot make in this environment.
A listening pass by the Director is the one check still outstanding.

## 6. Reproducibility

`render.sh` from a clean clone, with `ffmpeg` either on `PATH` or absent (the script bootstraps a
pinned static build when needed):

```
$ git clone https://github.com/dreamer2664/video-division.git && cd video-division
$ git checkout pixel/v01 && cd videos/team-a/pixel-v01
$ ./render.sh
```

Determinism: frames are a pure function of frame index (fixed seed 7 for texture, seed 42 for the
score); there is no wall-clock or random input anywhere in the picture path. The audio is synthesised
sample-by-sample from the same constants as the captions.

Byte-exactness caveat, stated honestly: **the container is not byte-identical across runs** because
the MP4 muxer writes a creation timestamp, and libx264's threaded rate control can shift a few
macroblocks. The *content* is deterministic — re-renders match on decoded stream hashes and on all
measured properties. Verification of a re-render:

```
$ ffmpeg -v error -i video.mp4 -map 0 -c copy -f streamhash -hash sha256 -
0,v,SHA256=<video stream hash>
1,a,SHA256=<audio stream hash>
```

Shipped file (see the commit that adds this QA.md for the exact values):

```
sha256(video.mp4) = <recorded after final render>
video stream hash = <recorded>
audio stream hash = <recorded>
```

## 7. Music and SFX — source and licence

**Everything is synthesised by `assets/generate.py` (numpy, 48 kHz, 60.000 s exactly). There are no
samples, loops, or recordings of any kind.** No third-party audio licence applies because no
third-party audio is used.

The score, by design section:

| element | how it is made |
|---|---|
| bed | Am–F–C–G pad, three detuned sine partials per voice, 8 s per chord, gentle attack/release |
| ticks | one per second, 1800 Hz, panned by dot position — the film's clock |
| fill shimmer | high ticks rate-derived from the same fill functions that drive the picture, so the sound *is* the animation |
| impacts | 55 Hz sine booms with a fast pitch drop at 18.05 / 25.2 / 34.15 / 43.95 s (the slams) |
| heartbeat | paired thumps at 36/38, 51/53/55 s under the lifetime section |
| master | summed, `tanh` soft-clip at 1.3 drive × 0.92, then peak-normalised to 0.706 |

Fonts are the only third-party files in the repo:

- `assets/fonts/DejaVuSans-Bold.ttf`, `DejaVuSansMono-Bold.ttf`
- **Licence:** DejaVu Fonts — Bitstream Vera / public-domain-derived free licence, full text in
  `assets/fonts/LICENSE-DejaVu.txt`. Vendored deliberately so `render.sh` needs no system fonts.

## 9. Self-review, blunt

**What works.** The arithmetic is the film. 10,000 dots × 100 seconds fills the frame, and the same
fabric, unchanged and uncounted differently, then means 11½ days, 31.7 years, 31,688 years, and
"mammoths were still here". The true-scale gag at 40–50 s — a billion seconds as a full-width bar
and a million as a sliver under 2 px — is the sharpest single image in it, and it is a picture only
code can draw at true proportions. The ending is honest rather than sentimental: "A human life is
about 2½ billion seconds. This film used 60 of yours."

**What is weaker.**
- It is typography over a grid for 60 seconds. That is the brief, but a viewer who does not read
  the first two sentences by 6 s has no reason to stay; the hook leans entirely on the type.
- S5's year sweep (1995 → 2026) is the least legible passage — a moving year label on a dark panel
  over a dense dot field. I fixed a collision and a panel occlusion, and it is readable, but it is
  the one section I would re-stage if I had another pass.
- The 1.6 px million sliver is drawn at 2 px with a leader line and labelled. That is an
  exaggeration of scale disclosed on screen, not a lie in the maths, but it *is* an approximation
  and the honest version would be a hairline that some displays cannot show.
- The S7 "this film" marker is a 4 px amber cap standing in for 60 s of a 79-year bar (true width
  0.00004 px). Same class of honest exaggeration, same disclosure.
- No narration. Decided deliberately — burned-in type is the narration — but it makes the piece
  dependent on reading speed, and captions exist partly as insurance for that choice.
- Loudnorm ran in **dynamic** mode (the material's 17 dB crest factor exceeds what linear gain can
  hit at −16 LUFS / −1.5 dBTP without clipping). It is gentle — LRA 4.2 LU, and the measured
  integrated result is exactly −16.0 — but a dynamic normaliser is doing gain riding, and on a
  quiet listening chain that may be audible as slight level movement. A linear pass would require
  compressing the impacts first, which would flatten the score's dynamics.

**What I would do with more time.** Give S5 a wider panel and a stationary year read-out with a
moving indicator; add one genuinely non-typographic image in S2 (the dots as a growing bar chart
rather than a counter); and get 30 s of the audio into a room with speakers.
