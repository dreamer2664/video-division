## Ten-line frame analysis
1. **Ref 01, opening frames:** a dark roofline and red-lit windows establish night before the 3:07 stamp; the first caption is short and immediately readable.
2. **Ref 01, alert insert:** the wide house cuts to a close phone/emergency-alert graphic; caption words arrive in small, timed chunks rather than a paragraph card.
3. **Ref 01, family sequence:** the alert's number is visualized with a framed family image, then room/bed views; inserts make the count legible without explaining everything in one shot.
4. **Ref 01, rule and escalation:** phone screen and nursery/bedroom views alternate; a rule is followed by incremental room details, then quiet/sound changes carry the suspense.
5. **Ref 01, late frames:** the hallway/door and a figure at the threshold are held longer; the phone message recontextualizes what the viewer saw, then the ending leaves a question/continuation cue.
6. **Ref 02, opening:** a 2:22 A.M. stamp and `COUNT YOUR FAMILY · PART 1` sit above a dark, straight-on hallway/bathroom-door composition; a small on-screen counter keeps the premise visible.
7. **Ref 02, middle:** cuts move among Ellie asleep, the dark window, and phone messages; the image changes on a caption beat, while the counter and timestamp persist as orientation.
8. **Ref 02, reveal/end:** the mother's message (`don't come in` / `your father doesn't snore`) shifts the meaning of the hallway silhouette; the final question/poll and part cue are a direct social hook.
9. **Ref 03, full sequence:** message bubbles alternate with a green-lit bed/phone view; short imperative captions and a 3:31 clock create a compact visual grammar, then the two-sided phone/door split makes the choice spatial.
10. **Across all three:** the 1-second contact sheets and extracted Release frame sets show clean, dark flat illustration, persistent night identifiers, large lower-third karaoke captions (usually one 2–5-word phrase), hard cuts, and slow pushes; audio is a constant bed under close narration with marked transient accents, then a pause/turn and a final hook.
