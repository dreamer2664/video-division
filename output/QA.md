## 4. Counter verification (driven by cue sheet, one value per frame)

`C(t) = t` hours (t in seconds), 1 h per film second. Named frames:

| Frame | Time | Counter (hours awake) | Caption beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.0 s | 0 h | "What if I never slept?" |
| 150 | 5.0 s | 5 h | "I stayed awake for you" |
| 300 | 10.0 s | 10 h | (counter only — voice paused) |
| 449 | 14.97 s | 14 h | (counter only) |

Counter changes every frame (value read straight from `counter_per_frame`); white-digit pixel counts differ at 0 s (11,152), 5 s (9,443), 10 s (18,814), 15 s (17,460) — confirming different digits are drawn, i.e. the number visibly ticks.

## 8. What I could not verify

- **Human naturalness of the voice:** no listening test performed (division research explicitly notes acoustic metrics cannot prove perceived naturalness).
- **Full-film (75 s) caption-to-word-boundary lock:** demonstrated on the 15 s proof only; full narration not synthesized this round (kept within the 1,500-char round cap; 432 / 1,500 used).
- **Per-frame counter OCR:** verified via white-digit pixel counts, not optical character recognition.
- **Loudness at −16 LUFS target:** this proof is a short stem; locked film would be mixed to −16 LUFS via `vocal_chain.py`.

## 9. Self-review ("what is weak")

- The presenter and sidekick are simple procedural placeholders (circles/rects); vox owns the real character animation.
- The 15 s proof pads an 8.13 s voice stem to 15 s with silence; a locked film would have continuous narration.
- Counter runs at a stated acceleration (1 film s = 1 real h); this is a filmic convention, flagged in the recipe.
- No music bed; the recipe permits low rhythmic music only if licensed/generated and logged.
